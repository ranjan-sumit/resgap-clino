Recommended Research Proposals

Multimodal Machine Learning Model Predicting Anxiety and Depression in Adults with Co-occurring Autism and ADHD Using Trait Measures and Normative Brain Imaging

Problem: Adults with co-occurring autism and ADHD (AuDHD) experience high rates of anxiety and depression, yet clinicians lack objective tools to identify those at greatest risk. Early detection is essential for timely intervention and improved quality of life.

Methodology: Retrospective cohort: merge the open UK/US adult dataset (4,996 participants) containing self‑report autism and ADHD trait scores with structural MRI scans processed through the Brain‑Charting centile pipeline (total gray matter volume, cortical surface area, etc.). Train a gradient‑boosted decision tree model (XGBoost) to predict clinically diagnosed internalising disorders (binary outcome). Perform nested cross‑validation, SHAP analysis for interpretability, and calibrate probabilities. Prospective validation: recruit 200 AuDHD adults from a neurodevelopmental clinic, obtain the same trait and MRI data, and assess model performance (AUROC, calibration) following TRIPOD guidelines.

Novelty: First integration of dimensional neurodiversity trait measures with normative neuroimaging centile scores to predict internalising comorbidities specifically in AuDHD. Builds on the trait‑based findings of "Neurodiversity and mental health in adulthood..." and the normative MRI centile framework of "Brain‑Charting Autism and Attention‑Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder..." which have never been combined.

Longitudinal Normative Modeling of Brain Development Trajectories in Children with Co-occurring Autism and ADHD

Problem: Current lifespan brain reference charts describe typical development and single diagnoses, but clinicians lack age‑specific neuroanatomical benchmarks for children with AuDHD, limiting early identification of atypical trajectories linked to functional outcomes.

Methodology: Identify AuDHD participants (n≈300) within large longitudinal MRI cohorts (ABCD, Generation R, NIH‑MRI). Apply GAMLSS‑based centile modeling to generate individualized brain‑age trajectories for cortical surface area, thickness, and subcortical volumes. Compare trajectories against typical‑development and single‑diagnosis (autism‑only, ADHD‑only) groups using mixed‑effects models with age‑by‑diagnosis interaction terms. Correlate deviation scores with standardized measures of executive function, academic achievement, and symptom severity at each wave.

Novelty: First application of lifespan centile modeling to a longitudinal AuDHD subgroup, extending the cross‑sectional centile work of "Brain‑Charting Autism and Attention‑Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder..." and providing dynamic biomarkers absent from existing literature.

Mendelian Randomization of Brain Imaging Phenotypes to Elucidate Causal Pathways Underlying Co-occurring Autism and ADHD

Problem: While GWAS and MR studies have identified causal brain‑structural links for ADHD and ASD separately, the shared and distinct causal neurobiological pathways driving AuDHD remain unknown, hindering mechanistic understanding and therapeutic targeting.

Methodology: Create a combined AuDHD GWAS meta‑analysis by harmonizing ADHD (iPSYCH, PGC) and ASD (PGC) summary statistics using MTAG. Use ENIGMA cortical surface area and white‑matter microstructure GWAS as exposure instruments. Perform two‑sample MR (IVW, MR‑Egger, weighted median) to test causal effects of specific imaging phenotypes on AuDHD risk. Conduct multivariable MR to adjust for overlapping ADHD/ASD effects and sex‑stratified MR to explore sex‑specific pathways. Validate findings with leave‑one‑out sensitivity and MR‑PRESSO for pleiotropy.

Novelty: Applies MR to a jointly defined AuDHD phenotype, a methodological step not taken in prior work. Integrates imaging GWAS from ENIGMA with the causal framework of "Causal Effects of Brain Structural Features on ADHD and ASD Risk..." to pinpoint shared versus disorder‑specific neurobiological drivers.

Equitable AI‑Driven Screening Tool for Anxiety and Depression in AuDHD within Primary Care Settings

Problem: Primary‑care providers often miss internalising disorders in patients with AuDHD due to overlapping symptomatology and lack of validated, culturally sensitive screening instruments, leading to delayed treatment.

Methodology: Develop a brief digital screening battery (10 items) combining validated autism and ADHD trait scales with two ultra‑short cognitive tasks (e.g., Stroop, Go/No‑Go). Train a calibrated logistic regression model on the open UK/US adult dataset (N=4,996) to predict clinician‑rated anxiety/depression diagnoses. Conduct bias audits across race, ethnicity, gender, and socioeconomic status. Prospective multi‑site validation in three primary‑care clinics serving diverse populations (n≈500 AuDHD adults). Follow STARD‑AI and TRIPOD reporting, obtain IRB approval, and ensure informed consent with plain‑language summaries.

Novelty: Creates the first equity‑focused, AI‑augmented screening instrument specifically validated for AuDHD in primary care, addressing the gap highlighted by the trait‑based internalising risk findings of "Neurodiversity and mental health in adulthood..." which lacked a practical screening solution.
