              Recommended Research Proposals

              Causally Informed Machine Learning for Predicting Anxiety and Depression in Neurodivergent Adults

              Problem: Current phenotypic models cannot distinguish whether autistic or ADHD traits causally drive internalising disorders, limiting personalized risk prediction. This work aims to integrate Mendelian Randomization (MR) causal estimates with supervised learning to produce mechanistically grounded predictions of anxiety and depression risk in adults with neurodivergent traits.

              Methodology: 1. Derive polygenic risk scores (PRS) for brain regions identified as causal in the MR study (superior frontal gyrus, lateral orbitofrontal gyrus, inferior fronto‑occipital fasciculus, retro‑lenticular internal capsule) using GWAS summary statistics. 2. Combine PRS with self‑report autism/ADHD trait scores (from the large UK/US cohort) as input features. 3. Train gradient‑boosted decision trees (XGBoost) and compare against baseline logistic regression. 4. Use nested cross‑validation and external validation on an independent cohort (e.g., All of Us). 5. Follow TRIPOD‑AI reporting standards and conduct calibration, discrimination, and decision‑curve analyses.

              Novelty: First framework that fuses MR‑derived causal effect sizes with individual‑level genetic and phenotypic data to predict internalising disorders, directly addressing the methodological gap of linking trait‑based phenotypes with MR estimates (gap: Integrate Mendelian Randomization with Machine Learning for Causal Prediction of Internalising Disorders).

              Neurodiversity Multi‑Ethnic Multimodal Cohort (NEMC): Linking Self‑Report Traits, EHR Diagnoses, MRI, and Genomics

              Problem: AI models for mental‑health risk lack robust, diverse training data that jointly contain phenotypic, clinical, imaging, and genetic information, restricting generalizability and fairness across populations.

              Methodology: 1. Obtain IRB approval and data‑use agreements with three partners: UK Biobank, All of Us, and a US academic health system (e.g., Mayo Clinic). 2. Extract self‑report autism/ADHD scales, EHR‑coded anxiety/depression diagnoses, brain MRI metrics (cortical surface area, white‑matter microstructure), and genome‑wide SNP data. 3. Harmonize variables using the OMOP Common Data Model and perform ancestry‑aware QC. 4. Create a de‑identified, tiered‑access repository with metadata describing provenance, consent, and linkage procedures. 5. Document dataset creation following FAIR principles and STARD‑AI reporting.

              Novelty: First publicly documented multimodal, multi‑ethnic dataset that integrates neurodivergent trait scores, clinical outcomes, neuroimaging, and genomics, directly filling the dataset gap identified (Create a Multi‑Ethnic, Multimodal Dataset Linking Self‑Report Traits, EHR Diagnoses, Imaging, and Genomics).

              Prospective Multi‑Site Validation of an AI‑Based Anxiety/Depression Risk Calculator for Adults with Autism and ADHD

              Problem: No prospective, multi‑institutional study has evaluated AI risk prediction tools for internalising disorders in neurodivergent adults, limiting evidence of real‑world utility and regulatory acceptance.

              Methodology: 1. Deploy the causally informed risk calculator (from Proposal 1) as a web‑service integrated with three health‑system EHRs (UK NHS Trust, US academic health system, and VA). 2. Enroll a prospective cohort of 2,000 neurodivergent adults (≥18 y) identified via ICD‑10 codes for ASD or ADHD. 3. Collect baseline trait scores, PRS, and covariates; compute risk scores at enrollment. 4. Follow participants for 12 months to capture incident clinically diagnosed anxiety or depression (ICD‑10 F40‑F41, F32‑F33). 5. Primary metrics: AUROC, calibration slope, net benefit (decision‑curve). 6. Conduct subgroup analyses (sex, ethnicity, SES) and report per TRIPOD‑AI, STARD‑AI, and CONSORT‑AI guidelines. 7. Perform a qualitative implementation study with clinicians to assess workflow impact.

              Novelty: First large‑scale, prospective, multi‑site validation of an AI risk model specifically for neurodivergent adults, addressing the evaluation gap (Prospective Multi‑Site Validation of AI Models for Anxiety/Depression Risk in Neurodivergent Adults).

              Explainable AI for Trait‑Based Prediction of Anxiety and Depression in Autism and ADHD

              Problem: Clinicians lack transparent tools that reveal how specific autistic and ADHD trait dimensions contribute to predicted internalising disorder risk, hindering trust and actionable decision‑making.

              Methodology: 1. Use the trained XGBoost model from Proposal 1. 2. Apply SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations) to quantify feature importance at the population and individual levels. 3. Generate counterfactual explanations (e.g., minimal trait score changes needed to shift risk below a clinical threshold). 4. Develop an interactive web dashboard that visualizes SHAP values and counterfactuals alongside patient‑level trait profiles. 5. Conduct a user‑study with psychiatrists and neurodevelopmental specialists to assess interpretability, trust, and perceived usefulness, following CONSORT‑AI reporting.

              Novelty: Introduces a clinician‑focused explainability pipeline for neurodivergent mental‑health risk prediction, filling the methodological gap (Explainable AI for Trait‑Based Prediction of Anxiety and Depression in Autism and ADHD) and extending beyond the odds‑ratio reporting of prior logistic models.
